Car vs cab vs metro: what your commute actually costs each month

farewise compares the true monthly cost of driving your own vehicle against cabs and metro/bus — for the trips you already take. Enter your distance, trips, fuel price and efficiency, parking, insurance, maintenance and average fares, and it names the cheapest mode and your monthly saving. Free, private and offline.

How the monthly math works

Monthly trips = trips per day × commute days. Monthly km = trips × distance. Fuel = km × price ÷ efficiency; the car's cost adds parking plus insurance ÷ 12 and maintenance ÷ 12. Cab = fare × trips. Metro/bus = fare × trips, capped by a monthly pass when the pass is cheaper.

A worked example

A 12 km commute, 2 trips a day, 22 days a month at ₹100/L and 15 km/L, with ₹1,000 parking and ₹12,000/yr each for insurance and maintenance, comes to ₹6,520/mo to drive. Cabs at ₹180/trip are ₹7,920/mo; a ₹1,000 metro pass is ₹1,000/mo. The metro wins — you'd save ₹6,920/mo (₹83,040/yr) versus cabs.

What it does not do

Fares are averages you enter — no surge or live pricing. It weighs rupees, not minutes. It assumes you already own the vehicle (no purchase, EMI or depreciation). Currency is a label only — no exchange-rate conversion.
